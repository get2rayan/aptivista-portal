## The two-vendor tax

Most companies hire a recruiter to find talent and a separate agency to build software. Each has its own account manager, its own contract, and its own definition of success. The handoff tax is invisible until you live it — misaligned expectations, duplicated discovery, and a gap between the people you hired and the work they were hired to do.

## One partner, one model

When staffing and delivery sit under one roof, a few things change. The team that scopes the work is the team that staffs it. The people who place engineers understand the stack they're hiring for because they build with it. Discovery happens once, not twice.

## Where it shows up

- **Onboarding** — the partner already knows the codebase conventions the new hire needs to learn.
- **Calibration** — screens reflect what the delivery team actually values, not a generic checklist.
- **Flexibility** — when a build needs more capacity, the same partner can augment; when a build winds down, talent can flow to the next engagement.

## The takeaway

Hiring a recruiter and an agency separately is sometimes necessary. But when you can have both under one roof, you remove a category of friction that quietly costs time and money on every engagement.
