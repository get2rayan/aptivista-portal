## The backfill problem

An engineer leaves. The roadmap slips. Hiring takes weeks you don't have. Backfilling a critical role fast — without lowering the bar — is a process problem, not a luck problem. Yet most teams treat it as a fire drill every time.

## The pipeline

A repeatable pipeline is what turns a fire drill into a calm, predictable motion:

1. **Requirement** — capture stack, seniority, team fit, and the line between must-haves and nice-to-haves. Vague requirements produce vague shortlists.
2. **Source** — work an active pipeline and a warm network first. Starting from a cold job ad is the slowest path.
3. **Screen** — run a structured technical screen *before* any client time is spent. Protect your interviewers' calendars.
4. **Shortlist** — present two to three vetted candidates with a one-page rationale each.
5. **Interview** — coordinate client rounds and keep the total loop under a week.
6. **Onboard** — prepare before day one so the new hire starts productive, not buried in paperwork.

## What actually buys you speed

Speed in backfilling doesn't come from cutting corners. It comes from removing friction:

- A **pre-warmed bench**, not a cold job ad.
- **One person owning the loop** end to end.
- **Structured screens** so clients only meet qualified people.
- A **documented onboarding checklist** per client.

## The takeaway

A 30-day backfill is realistic when the pipeline is already running. The companies that backfill fastest aren't luckier — they're organized. Build the pipeline once and every future backfill gets cheaper.
